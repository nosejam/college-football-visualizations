Victory cycles, “circles of suck” & search guarantees

Elo rings: red is the lowest and green the highest rating among all teams in the selected scope/week; amber is the midpoint. Filtering to a team or cycle does not rescale the colors. Every team starts each season at 1500. Completed games are replayed in kickoff order using win/loss Elo (K = 32, scale = 400), with no home-field or score-margin adjustment. Ties count as half a win. All available opponents’ games contribute to their ratings; the data coverage varies by division and season. Ring values are week-end ratings, not pregame predictions. The proof panel also shows both teams’ Elo immediately after that specific game.

Victory cycles are the familiar fan concept often called circles of suck. This project adds interactive season/week controls, multiple required teams, and browsable, verified cycles. Select every team you want to include. All small result sets are listed; larger searches show the five longest and five shortest cycles found. A time-limited list may be incomplete. If no matching cycle is found, one selected team shows its immediate opponents; multiple selected teams show shortest played-game connections, with arrows always pointing from winner to loser.

Each arrow points from a winner to a loser. Results are cumulative through the selected week. Select a team to show its entire connected group, so a circle can span many hops. Use 1 hop to show only direct opponents, then expand one hop at a time if you want a smaller neighborhood. Neighborhoods follow games in either direction, while circle searches always respect the arrow direction. Search uses the explored network scope. Opening a circle keeps that scope; Back to network restores its full view. With a team selected, “Must include” requires that team to belong to the circle; turn it off to search anywhere in the neighborhood.

A circle is a simple directed cycle: no team repeats, and every team beat the next, with the final team beating the first. By default circles can contain two or more teams, including A → B → A rematch pairs. Turn off “Allow circles of 2+ teams” to require at least three. Neither setting caps the maximum length. For a particular size, enter a number and use Find this length; Another circle browses the matching results. Games can occur in any chronological order, but every result must already be available by the chosen week. Repeated wins over the same opponent share one arrow; the proof panel shows scores and dates.

Proof status is explicit. With zero or one required team, shortest cycles are exact. With several required teams, a bounded search can leave the shortest or longest size unproven. Full-FBS longest-circle references are computed offline using an integer solver with subtour elimination, with bounds and proof status saved for every season/week. A reference is used only when all of its teams and wins are present and it satisfies any selected-team requirement. Other searches run in a background worker with a time budget. “Proven longest” means optimality is established; “Best found” includes a valid upper bound and can be improved with Search longer. A result is never labeled a proven maximum solely because the search timer ran out. Finding the longest simple cycle is computationally difficult, so a filtered or expanded graph may remain unproven.

FBS mode includes only that season’s FBS programs and games between them. “FBS + their opponents” also includes schools that played an FBS team by the selected week, plus available games among those schools. This is not a complete all-divisions network. Preseason has no results. Source: the same saved SportsDataverse ESPN/CFBD schedules as the imperialism map, including postseason and the 2020 spring games. Ties and unfinished games do not create arrows. Weeks run Tuesday–Monday in Eastern time, with Week 1 containing Labor Day weekend; postseason numbering continues. All assets and search code are embedded for offline use.
